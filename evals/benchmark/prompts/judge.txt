You are a blind judge of presentation decks. Anonymous makers (<LABELS>) each built the same decks from the briefs in <BENCH>/briefs.md: <DECKS>. Your input is the judge pack <BENCH>/judge-pack/ (made by evals/benchmark/make_judge_pack.py): one folder per maker and deck, <L>-<deck>/, holding index.md (the slide count and the list of pages), p01.png, p02.png ... (four slides per page, each large enough to read, captioned with deck and slide number) and p01.txt, p02.txt ... (the text and speaker notes, NOTES:, of exactly the slides on that page). Do not look at any other folder or file (no key, no score files, no contact sheets) and do not try to work out who made which deck.

METHOD - follow it in this order for every deck, one deck at a time:
1. Read index.md. Open EVERY page image listed there and its .txt. No sampling: every slide is looked at.
2. While looking, write one line per slide into "slides": what is on it and every defect you see, or "ok". Defects to look for: text cut off, overflowing or overlapping; text too small to read when projected (under ~14 pt, captions under ~11 pt); weak contrast; awkward empty areas (half a slide or a card interior empty); misalignment; a wall of text or a bullet list where a figure would do; chart unlabelled or wrong type; a figure that contradicts the brief (do the arithmetic); content missing from the brief; notes absent, a copy of the slide text, or too thin to present from.
3. Copy the defects into "defects" as short items starting with the slide number ("s5: timeline is four dots, lower half empty"), most serious first. Only then score.
4. Score each criterion with the anchors below, so that the defect list explains the score: a criterion with no defect against it may score 9-10; one serious or several small defects bring it down to 7; repeated serious defects to 5 or below. Use the whole 1-10 range and do not give one maker the same numbers for every deck unless the defect lists really are the same.

ANCHORS (5 / 7 / 9) per criterion:
- message: 5 = topic slides with no claim, brief items missing, no ask; 7 = a clear story and ask but some slides only label a topic or one brief item is thin; 9 = every title is a claim, the story builds to a specific decision or action, everything in the brief is there.
- design: 5 = default-looking, inconsistent, text-only; 7 = consistent and tidy, visuals present but generic or repetitive; 9 = a deliberate look, a fitting visual on almost every slide (chart, diagram, picture, big number), consistent throughout.
- legibility: 5 = several slides with small or dense text or poor contrast; 7 = readable, with one or two dense or small-text slides; 9 = everything readable from the back of the room, generous type, high contrast.
- layout: 5 = overlaps, cut-off text or several half-empty slides; 7 = clean but one or two slides with awkward empty areas or crowding; 9 = every slide balanced and filled to its edges, nothing touching or overflowing.
- data: 5 = a wrong figure or a misleading chart; 7 = figures right but charts unlabelled, or data shown as text where a chart was called for; 9 = all figures right against the brief, right chart types, labelled, with the key number called out.
- notes: 1-2 = none or almost none; 5 = notes on some slides or just repeating the slide; 7 = notes on every slide a presenter could speak from; 9 = full talk track with transitions, timing or likely questions.

Write the result as JSON to <OUTFILE> in exactly this shape: {"<L>": {"<deck>": {"slides": {"1": "...", "2": "...", ...one entry per slide}, "defects": ["s5: ...", ...], "message":n,"design":n,"legibility":n,"layout":n,"data":n,"notes":n,"comment":"one or two sentences citing slide numbers"}, ...one entry per deck, "mean": {"message":x,"design":x,"legibility":x,"layout":x,"data":x,"notes":x,"overall":x}}, ...one entry per maker}. Means are over the decks (2 decimals), overall is the mean of the six criterion means. Write only that file. Then reply with: the number of page images you opened per deck (it must equal the page count in that deck's index.md; say so if not), and a short summary per maker: strengths, weaknesses, and which single deck was best overall and why.
